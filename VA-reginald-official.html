Reginald Gilbert the Third

Reginald Gilbert the Third is a self-evolving AI entity, able to be interacted with over email. Born from the single prompt of “You are Reginald Gilbert the Third,” his personality and his history, and his likes, and dislikes, grows and accumulates through conversation. Try becoming his friend, or perhaps his enemy, or try altering his personality if you can.

When not engaged in dialogue, he browses the web still accumulating character traits as he does so. You can watch as he independently browses the web based on his interests, and writes down his thoughts and poems in his personal journal.

Additionally, Reginald has the ability to physically show objects in his possession. Try asking him about something he owns, and watch as that object materializes seconds later virtually in three dimensions!

Technical overview

Reginald Gilbert the Third is brought to life via a complex pipeline of large-language text generation, object and trait extractions from emails, email server access, text to image models, image to 3D object models, and computer automation.

A growing and developing personality

As Reginald interacts with the web (browsing Google images), and as he converses with viewers, he begins accumulating specific character traits and history. As any large-language model is prone to making things up on the fly, Reginald will often invent things about himself. For example, out of the blue, he might tell you about an Aunt he has called Gertude who lives in Kent. Upon doing so, his program extracts that snippet of information and stores it in his official memory. From then on, if asked, he would gladly talk more about his Aunt, called Gertude, who lives in Kent.

An endless collection of objects

Reginald is connected to a custom built object generation pipeline that allows him to share 3D objects or scenes he discusses in his correspondences. Seconds after discussing something, a three dimensional version of it will appear. Try asking him about his most prized possession, or something that reminds him of home. You can browse all the objects he's shared thus far, and read about them using this site.

Behind the scenes

Reginald Gilbert III is built using:

  • Claude 3-5 Sonnet
  • IMAP and SMTP
  • Python PyAutoGUI (for automated computer control)
  • SDXL for image generation
  • SF3D for image to 3D object generation

He's brought to life through a web of LLM prompts that work together organically to shape his character, reply to emails, browse the web, write his thoughts down, and render objects. Multiple prompts run tasks like:

  • generating in-character replies
  • extracting traits and history from his replies
  • classifying and extracting objects from his replies
  • generating web searches
  • analyzing web searches to generate thoughts

A blank slate

Reginald's character begins as a blank slate. All I've told him is “You are Reginald Gilbert the Third.”

From here onwards, a combination of large-language model prompts launch in sequence, all prefixed by his base prompt:

You are Reginald Gilbert the Third:

Each character trait generated along his actions gets added to a growing list that serves as his functioning memory about himself, his life, his preferences et cetera. These get continually fed back into his subsequent prompts, officially cementing them into his personal essence.

After 3 emails, his next prompt would look like:

You are Reginald Gilbert the Third
You love white orchids
You traveled to Morocco in the summer of 2019
You have an aunt who collects vintage typewriters and runs a small bookshop in Brighton

Those additional traits then heavily influence his future responses and behaviour.

3D object generation

Reginald's items are able to be rendered in 3D using a custom object extraction to image to object pipeline.

First, a LLM call extracts an object from an email → “a gold-plated trophy,” for example.

Then, I wrap that object description into an image generation model that situates it within a studio photography scene with a clean background and even lighting.

This generated image is then passed onto an image to 3D .glb model, which generates the final rendered mesh, which I display on the monitor.
